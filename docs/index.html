Strategy Overview

A systematic, vol-targeted long/short book across four energy futures, rebalanced weekly.

Each week, four commodities — CL (WTI Crude), NG (Henry Hub Natural Gas), RB (RBOB Gasoline), and HO (Heating Oil / ULSD) — are scored on three signals and combined into a single composite z-score per commodity:

  • Momentum — average of 12- and 26-week trailing returns.
  • Carry — trailing price vs. rolling-mean proxy for term-structure shape (a stand-in for calendar-spread carry, since only front-month continuous contracts are available from this data source).
  • Seasonality — historical same-calendar-month return, using only data available up to that point.

The composite signal is converted to a position via tanh-squashed conviction sizing, scaled to a 15% annualized volatility target per commodity, capped at 2x leverage. Positions are lagged one week (trade on signal at week t, realize the return over week t→t+1) and charged 5bps transaction cost on turnover.

Data quality notes

CL (WTI) printed -$37.63 on 2020-04-20 — a real historical event (the physical-delivery expiry squeeze), not corrupted data. It broke pct_change-based math everywhere it touched CL: momentum, carry, and seasonality signals in signals.py, plus the P&L and vol-targeting calculations in backtest.py. Every one of those five call sites was switched to a negative-price-safe formula — raw price differences for the signal functions (scale-invariant once z-scored), and (Pt − Pt-1) / |Pt-1| for the return series that feed position sizing and portfolio P&L (mathematically identical to pct_change for positive prices, but well-defined and correctly signed for any price).

Raw return vs. Sharpe — how much was really the hurdle rate?

Sharpe subtracts RISK_FREE_RATE (4%/yr). Verified algebraically and numerically that this is applied correctly relative to the weekly holding period (no double-penalization bug). The question is how much of a "negative Sharpe" reflects genuine losses vs. a positive-but-sub-4% return.

Key Findings

Four things worth knowing before trusting this strategy with real risk.

Not systematic · outlier-driven

Carry's biggest bets on CL/RB are fine — the story is COVID timing, not skill

Pulling the 10 largest-|z-score| carry weeks for CL and RB shows a coin-flip hit rate (50%), yet positive cumulative contribution (+3.09% CL, +4.77% RB over just those 10 weeks). A single correctly-timed short entered 2020-03-13 — right before the COVID crude crash — contributed +6.8% (CL) and +5.5% (RB) on its own. The signal isn't reliably right at the extremes; it just got one macro regime-shift dead right.

Systematically wrong

Heating Oil's largest carry bets are genuinely bad

Same test on HO tells a different story: 7 of its 10 largest-|z| weeks were wrong, and the wrong ones weren't small (2020-04-24: -4.0%; 2026-01-30: -3.95%; 2025-06-20: -2.93%). Unlike CL/RB, this isn't a couple of outliers skewing an otherwise-fine average — it's a real pattern worth trimming or excluding high-|z| carry bets on HO specifically.

Diagnosed and fixed

Vol-targeting undershot its 15% target by 4x — fixed, Sharpe flipped positive

Realized portfolio vol was only 3.72% against a 15% target. Ruled out the two obvious suspects: the 2x leverage cap never bound (0% of weeks, even after correcting), and the 12-week rolling vol estimator wasn't biased low (full-conviction legs landed at 17–19%, right at target). The actual cause: tanh(signal / 2.0) conviction sizing — the composite signal's typical magnitude (mean |z| ≈ 0.47) rarely nears the tanh's saturation point, so average conviction was only ~22% of "full." A further ~1.5x reduction came from genuine cross-commodity diversification (NG correlates negatively with the other three legs). Fixed with a calibrated CONVICTION_SCALE = 4.03 multiplier in backtest.py, confirmed end-to-end.

Signal / commodity mismatch

Momentum and carry don't transfer to natural gas — seasonality does, relatively

NG is the weakest leg under every signal, but not uniformly: momentum (Sharpe -0.70) and carry (-1.07) are both badly negative, while seasonality (-0.16) is far closer to breakeven and is the only signal with a positive correlation to NG's forward returns (+0.044, vs. -0.046 for momentum and -0.055 for carry). NG's storage-driven, weather-seasonal supply/demand dynamics plausibly explain why a calendar-based signal reads it better than trend- or curve-shape-based ones. Testing exclusion (Sharpe 0.44) vs. half-weight (0.43) vs. quarter-weight (0.43) vs. current full inclusion (0.41) showed a smooth, monotonic tradeoff — more NG weight reduction raises Sharpe/return but also raises drawdown roughly in step; no option dominates outright.

Run It Yourself

Everything above is reproducible from the repo — no hidden state.

# from the repo root
venv\Scripts\python.exe src\data_pipeline.py   # pull/cache prices + EIA storage
venv\Scripts\python.exe src\signals.py         # sanity-check the composite signal
venv\Scripts\python.exe src\backtest.py        # full backtest: strategy vs. buy & hold
venv\Scripts\python.exe src\main.py            # end-to-end run, writes output/ (metrics CSVs + equity_curve.png)

Requires EIA_API_KEY in a .env file at the repo root (loaded automatically by data_pipeline.py) — free key at eia.gov/opendata/register.php. Without it, storage data is skipped gracefully and only the price-driven signals run.
